# Authoring DEAD AIR

This is a personal place to return to and find something worth spending a few minutes with. It has a point of view, but the form of the next addition should remain a surprise.

## The point of view

The terminal is industrial and a little hostile. What happens inside it can be human: remembering a person, making a sound, following a strange link, solving something, or getting a small piece of your life in order. Specific details do more work than lore, invented departments, or a screen full of fake diagnostics.

Dead Drop establishes one thread: ordinary acts of agency against systems that erase things. Its parcels are a voice, sunlight, and an address. Future fiction can continue that thread without requiring every tool or experiment to inhabit the same city.

Avoid corporate dashboards, personality recaps, forced whimsy, and decorating a generic utility with cyberpunk vocabulary. Write concretely. A cracked aquarium tells us more than a paragraph about a dystopian megacity.

## What counts as a release

Ship a new thing with an idea behind it: a game, a comic installment, an instrument, a useful tool, an image study, an interactive essay, a puzzle, or a deliberately useless experiment. Give it a title and a satisfying amount of substance. A two-minute game can have a beginning, a challenge, and an ending.

The persistent relay supplies navigation and storage. Each release can have its own typography, color, composition, and interaction. The next release should be free to look and behave differently from Dead Drop. Repeated theme changes alone do not satisfy the daily-update idea.

Useful directions for a future author to choose from:

- **Night Maintenance:** a short ongoing comic about a worker repairing a city that keeps changing its own plans. One finished scene per installment; keep every previous page readable.
- **Borrowed Window:** two original images of the same place at different times, with a small interaction that reveals what changed.
- **Exit Cost:** a useful, local-only tool for comparing the time and money a recurring obligation consumes. Plain language and actual calculations.
- **One Bad Sound:** a playable sound experiment with one constraint and a way to keep a recording or preset.

These are directions, not shipped features or a promised calendar. Choose the idea that can be finished well; surprise comes from varied authored additions.

## The front door and the archive

The root URL opens the newest release. Keep its main interaction close to its introduction, especially on a phone. The archive links directly to older work and displays real saved progress where useful. Existing utility channels stay directly reachable.

Record actual release numbers and publication dates. Preserve past work rather than replacing it with a new homepage theme. Use occasional narrative connections as rewards for returning; never make a new visitor read an encyclopedia before playing.

## Continuity and delivery

Saved runs, receipts, notes, and creative work should survive new releases. Extend the versioned save format additively; accept older backups, validate imports, and include the new records in export. Keep processing local unless a future feature explicitly needs a service. Browser saves do not sync between phones and computers.

Verify the new interaction, reload persistence, backup restoration, and small-screen controls. Check existing behavior when shared storage or navigation changes. A release should work from the GitHub Pages repository subpath.

Daily production requires a separately configured development schedule. There is no automated authoring or publishing job connected today. Rotating a date, seed, or item from a fixed pool must not be presented as shipping a new feature.
